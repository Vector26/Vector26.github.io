Know Thyself, or Be Told Who You Are

Stop borrowing personalities. Go find your own.

Here I am listening to ‘Bad Idea’ by Girl In Red, on my IEMs with a really thought through DAC. If you don’t know what any of these items mean, don’t worry, even I didn’t some months ago. Well, let’s just say for the sake of this article that they are a pathway/tunnel to finding what my taste is and what I actually like in life.

We are often raised as a part of society and expected to perform in a manner that keeps the machine going, almost like a spare part for other tools until they retire. Well, for me, that has always been a bit of an anomaly, you know, because we are called individuals but expected to be more or less the same as the next human. Of course, not everyone tries to blend in and thank God for it.

In today’s time, when creativity seems to be at an all-time low on average but the noise around it is at an all-time high, one needs to show more individuality. Whether it be music taste, art, actual food taste or a career path. There are more people trying to be different, but somehow the quality of that individuality has gone down. Everyone is picking up some curated aesthetic from reels or copying a niche persona just to look different on paper, when in reality nobody is sitting alone with their own head long enough to figure out what they actually like when no one is watching.

The internet has been a big normaliser for it. It bombards us with information and endless consumption until we are just livestock for platforms to suck the thinking out of. Finding and following trends has never been so easy, and getting influenced by them has become almost second nature. And it all caters to this generation’s aggravated FOMO. We are so scared of being left out that we just borrow someone else’s personality to fill the void. I might be sounding like an old uncle right now, saying it’s all this generation’s fault type stuff, but unfortunately it is the responsibility of this generation, without it being their fault. We didn’t break the system, we just spawned into it, which sucks, but no one else is coming to fix it for us.

There is an old quote that goes like: “The world will ask you who you are and if you don’t know, then it will tell you what it needs you to be”. I think this pretty much suggests that a person needs to know themselves, otherwise they will eventually be assigned a label externally. And obviously the world is only gonna give you a label that makes you a good spare part for its own machine, not something that necessarily makes you happy. This also points towards knowing how to love yourself, because otherwise you will keep getting attracted to things that are unhealthy for you. If you don’t know what you want, you will just end up chasing whatever cheap validation or toxic people give you a quick dopamine hit.

Now, if you are thinking how is this even important or, you know, how does any of this matter to you, then maybe answering a few questions will help:

What are you chasing in your life? And why that particular thing? What would your life look like with that thing in hand? Will it give you eternal rest? And then what? Another race?

If the above got you thinking for more than 5 min, then maybe you should read ahead.

No, this isn’t some black-pilled advice for this generation to develop fake confidence or anything. Though this does require a certain amount of courage to read

To be bold is to simply be unapologetic about who you are. The world doesn’t reward good slavery, but an unaccommodating customer who yells and screams for what they deserve. [This in no way means that you should be unkind and use boldness as an alibi for it. There is a difference between standing up for yourself and being an ass.]

A person only attracts when they don’t chase. And I think the chase is pretty much over once they know who they are and don’t have this constant need to become something else as a form of conformity. You stop trying to convince everyone that you are someone worth noticing, and ironically, that is probably when people start noticing.

Conformity does work, and it should be done as a sacrifice for people you feel are worth sacrificing for, without expecting anything in return. Being yourself doesn’t mean you become so rigid that you cannot make room for anyone else. But there is a difference between making compromises for someone you care about and continuously changing yourself just to be accepted by people. Be intentional about your things

Knowing yourself would require a lot of activities to be done, and usually would get you a tag of selfish, but it is important to know why you are doing this in the first place. You cannot save/help someone unless you are capable of saving yourself. [This simply means that you have to be so much bigger in the situation when you are saving someone that you don’t even feel any issue in helping them. Like giving sugar to an ant.]

Something people tend to undervalue is learning what makes them volatile and understanding how much of it they can actually deal with. If I have certain triggers that I know are there and have spent years fighting them, then I will simply keep my life evading those triggers. I don’t see the point in repeatedly putting myself through something just to prove that I am okay with it. It doesn’t help anyone if I am pretending I am okay, especially not myself.

Now let’s come to the how part. Five words: Fuck around and find out. Not in the literal sense, but in the general sense. You need to try things that you can, to find what all do you like. As Robert Frost said, to take the road not taken.

I have tried and dusted several things after I moved to Bangalore, and every month I realised more and more of who I am, was, and will be (inherently). I tried new things which I didn’t like at first, but after a while they became my favourite. I rekindled my spark for playing tennis, found that I don’t enjoy MMA as much as I thought I would, understood the type of people I wanna hang out with, which people trigger which parts of me, etc.

All in all, go out and have an open mind about things. Pattern recognition is good for safety, but if you ain’t taking risks, you are just a bag of bones, muscles, and blood being carried away where the universe takes you.

Once you explore and rest, you will realise how comforting and secure a feeling it is to know exactly what your inner self wants. Maybe it will also help you understand and make you live with the ‘great void’ inside you.
